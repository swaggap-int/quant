      How to read this

      One run at a time

      Every backtest you save becomes a JSON file in results/, and this page lists them newest first. Pick one from the run list on the left — or from the Run dropdown on a narrow window — and the whole page below re-reads it. Nothing here re-runs a backtest; it reads what was already saved.

      The equity curve

      The solid amber line is the strategy. The dashed line is buy & hold — the same money left in the asset for the same window, with no fees. The band underneath is the drawdown: how far below its own peak the account sat at each moment. If the amber line never beats the dashed one, the strategy made money, and less of it than doing nothing would have.

      Hover anywhere on the curve and the bar's date, close, both equity values and the drawdown appear above the chart. Move off it and the readout returns to the final bar.

      Three vertical scales, and why

      • Both series fits the strategy and buy & hold on one axis. Honest, but a curve trailing buy & hold ten-fold gets flattened into a straight line.
      • Strategy only fits the axis to the strategy alone, so its own shape is legible. When buy & hold runs off the top it is left undrawn, and the footer says so.
      • Logarithmic gives equal room to equal percentage moves, which is the fair way to compare a curve that grew 10× against one that grew 44×.

      The control sits in the rail on a wide window and above the chart on a narrow one.

      The footer under the chart always names the scale in use. A scale change is never silent.

      The verdict line

      Above the chart, one sentence judges the run against a fixed rulebook: fewer than 20 trades, a drawdown deeper than 50%, a profit factor under 1.2, fees eating the return, or failing to beat buy & hold. If it starts with Read with care, at least one of those fired. If it does not, that means there were no red flags in this window — which is not the same as an edge. Several windows and markets is what run_robustness.py is for.

      The spec sheet

      Four groups of numbers: Returns (total, CAGR, buy & hold, excess, final equity, window length), Risk (max drawdown, longest time underwater, volatility, Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar, time in market), Trades (count, win rate, profit factor, expectancy, best and worst, average hold in bars and in R multiples) and Costs (fees paid, fees as a share of starting equity, turnover, round-trip friction).

      Green and red are direction, never magnitude. Read the number.

      Reading the trade log

      Round trips are completed positions: entry, exit, size, the price it went in and out at, net PnL after fees and slippage, the result in R multiples, how far it ran for and against you (MFE / MAE), and why it closed. Fills are the raw orders, including stops that ratcheted. A trade with a huge return and two bars held is noise, not a finding — check the count before believing the average.

      Running a new backtest

      New run opens a form pre-filled from the run you are looking at. Change the symbol, timeframe or strategy settings and press Run backtest. Shorts assume 100% margin with no funding cost; the slippage box is the assumption most likely to flatter a result, so do not set it to zero unless you are only testing signal quality. Long histories need a first fetch, which is the slow part; after that the candles are cached on disk and repeat runs finish in about a second.

      Reading a curve with the keyboard

      A mouse is not required. The list under the chart repeats these, and none of them fire while you are typing in a field:

      • ← → step one bar at a time. The readout above the chart follows, and the crosshair marks the bar you are on.
      • Shift with either arrow jumps a twentieth of the whole series, which is how you cross a decade of daily bars without holding a key down.
      • j and k move to the next and previous saved run, so you can flip through a folder of results without touching the list.
      • Esc clears the marker and puts the readout back on the final bar.
      • ? opens this guide, and Esc closes it.
      • y copies the bar you scrubbed to — date, close, strategy equity and drawdown — so you can paste it anywhere.

      While the chart has the keyboard it draws an amber outline around itself, so you can tell scrubbing apart from a stray keypress.

      Theme

      The toggle in the top bar switches light and dark and remembers your choice; with nothing stored, it follows your operating system. Animation and the pointer ring switch themselves off when your system asks for reduced motion.
